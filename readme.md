# Iterated Rationality Models with Lexical Uncertainty
## Abstract
Recent works present models of game-theoretic approaches to derive Scalar Implicatures (SI), within the neo-Gricean approach, which can be referred as Iterated Rationality Models (IRM). In particular, the Rational Speech Act (RSA) framework assumes a probabilistic inference game between a pragmatic speaker and listener. One of the successful versions of RSA are of Bergen, Levy, and Goodman (2016), assuming compositional lexical uncertainty. However, a lexicalist account for SI is prone to internal conflicts, such as prediction of suspension under negation, divergence in DE contexts, wrong predictions for two-upper-bound triggering expressions and for ellipsis. A naive lexical ambiguity approach does not seem to escape these problems. In this work, I review the lexical uncertainty approach for neo-Gricean pragmatics within the IRM framework. For simplicity, I assume the IRM framework of Fox and Katzir (2021). I define a lexical uncertainty implementation of Fox and Katzir (2021), and test the predictions of this model for various implicatures that were suggested to challenge the lexicalist account.
